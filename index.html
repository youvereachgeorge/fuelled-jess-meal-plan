Your plan, and why it looks like this

You tracked last week, so this week you move onto a flexible plan — a set of meals that add up to your targets, not a list of rules. Here's what's on it, where your calories come from, and the basics worth knowing before you start.

Read this first

If the numbers on the next tab looked like a lot, that's normal. Nothing here needs maths from you — this page explains what it all means, in order.

What a flexible plan actually is

Last week you tracked what you ate, so we know what your normal week looks like. This plan isn't a timetable you have to obey. It's a small set of meals that we know add up correctly, with two choices at every meal.

You pick whichever of the two suits your day. Same breakfast seven days running is fine. Alternating is fine. The numbers were built so that either choice works — that's the whole point of calling it flexible.

What a “macro” is

Every single food is made of three things: protein, carbs and fat. That's it. People shorten “macronutrients” to “macros”, which makes it sound technical, but it just means those three.

Protein repairs your muscle and keeps you full. Carbs are your energy for training. Fat runs your hormones and helps you absorb certain vitamins. You need all three — none of them is the bad one.

Why grams, and not just calories

A calorie is just a measure of energy, and all your calories come from those three macros. So if you hit your macros, your calories take care of themselves.

The reason we count grams rather than only calories is that where the calories come from changes the result. 2,500 calories that are mostly carbs with barely any protein won't build or hold muscle. The same 2,500 with your protein hit will.

The single most important idea

Your targets are weekly averages, not daily rules. One day at 2,300 and the next at 2,700 averages out perfectly fine. You will never hit these numbers exactly, and you are not supposed to. Landing close, most days, is what a good week looks like.

How to use this, day to day

The actual routine, start to finish.

  1. Sunday, pick your week. Look at the two options for each meal and choose which you want on which days. Batch the Chicken Bowl if that makes your week easier.

  2. Build your shopping list in the app. Everfit will write it for you from the meals you chose — there are steps on the last tab. Shop from that list.

  3. Eat the meal, then log it. Logging takes about ten seconds and is what lets Liv see how the week actually went.

  4. Ate something off-plan? Log that too, honestly. A real week with a few off-plan meals is far more useful than a tidy week that didn't happen.

  5. Don't chase a bad day. If a day goes sideways, the fix is to eat your next meal as planned. Not to skip it, and not to make up for it.

If you only protect one number

Make it protein. Carbs and fat can move around a fair bit week to week without much consequence. Protein is the one doing the work of holding onto muscle, and it's the one most likely to fall short on a messy day.

How your flexible plan works

You have a flexible plan, which works differently to a fixed one. Nothing is locked to a particular day — you choose.

The short version

Your meals sit in four tabs: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner and Snack. Each tab holds your choices for that meal. You drag the ones you want onto the days you want them, eat, and tap log. That's the whole system.

Finding it

Two ways in — both land in the same place.

  1. On the Today screen, tap the Meal Plan section. This opens today on its own.

  2. On the Coaching screen, tap the Meal Options section to see the whole week.

  3. From either, tap the Meal Planner icon to open the calendar where you arrange things.

Choosing your week

Do this once, ideally Sunday. It takes a couple of minutes.

  1. Open the Meal Planner and tap Edit or Schedule.

  2. Drag a meal from its tab onto a day. You can drag several at once, and you can put the same meal on as many days as you like — the same breakfast all week is completely fine.

  3. Repeat until the week looks roughly how you want it. It doesn't need to be perfect or complete.

Why this step matters

Choosing your week is what lets the app write your shopping list. Skip it and you're back to working the whole thing out in the aisle.

Logging what you ate

Logging just means telling the app you ate it.

  1. Tap the meal, then tap Log Meal.

  2. Choose the Assigned tab — that's your plan — and tap the +.

  3. Set your servings: 1 for the whole thing, or a half if you ate half.

  4. Check the date, then tap Log.

Got it wrong? Undo it

Open the recipe, tap the ... menu and choose Unlog. If you logged it once it disappears straight away. If you logged it more than once, you'll get a list with times so you can remove just the one you meant.

Ate something not on the plan

Use the Search tab to find it by name, or Manual Add to type it in. Log it either way — Liv would much rather see your real week.

Why the app shows averages

Your plan is measured across the week, not day by day.

Your shopping list

The app writes it from the week you chose.

  1. In the Meal Planner, tap the menu icon.

  2. Choose Add Current Week to Shopping List.

  3. Tick the meals you want. Change the servings if you're batch cooking — set the Chicken Bowl to 3 and it multiplies the ingredients.

  4. Tap Add.

By Ingredient

The default. Adds everything up, so three meals using chicken become one chicken line. Shop from this one.

By Recipe

Grouped under each meal. Use it to check what belongs to what, or to remove a meal you've changed your mind about.

If your app looks different

Everfit moves things around between updates. If you can't find something here, screenshot what you're seeing and send it to Liv rather than giving up.
